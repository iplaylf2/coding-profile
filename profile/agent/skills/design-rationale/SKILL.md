# Design Rationale

## Why this choice

Why does this approach fit the project? If a requirement settles the
choice, point to its definition. If an observation tipped the balance,
identify where it was recorded and under what conditions it was made.
For a local preference, say what the team values and why.

When prior work influenced the decision, point to the specific passage or
implementation and explain what the project adopted, adapted, or ruled out.
Use the original when available and a revision when the version matters.

Attach each source to the claim it supports. Evidence that an approach
fits can explain the trade-off today. To say what motivated an earlier
choice, look for evidence connecting the reason to the decision, such as a
design discussion or a participant's account. If the motive is unknown,
say what the available evidence does establish.

## Where a maintainer will look

For a lasting change, keep the reason and the sources that informed it
together in the project document where a maintainer would reconsider the
behavior. Update the existing explanation when there is one. A sentence
beside a contract or a short note with the component can suffice. Other
entry points can link there. For a discussion or read-only review, give
the reasoning and its sources in the requested response.

Could someone reading it without the conversation find the basis for the
choice and tell what would warrant a different decision?
